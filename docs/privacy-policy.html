Phone Doctor

Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 28, 2026 · Applies to Phone Doctor 2.1.0 and later until this page is revised.

Phone Doctor is designed around local, on-device diagnostics. The App does not require an account and does not operate a developer-controlled backend for storing your diagnostic history.
Package: com.phonedoctor.app

1. Local diagnostics and local storage

Phone Doctor ("the App") is an Android device diagnostics utility. Most diagnostic work is performed locally using Android system APIs and device hardware interfaces.

Diagnostic results, scan history, settings, consent-related app state, and the temporary one-hour ad-free reward state are stored in Android app-private storage on your device. Phone Doctor does not upload your diagnostic history to a developer-controlled server.

2. Information Phone Doctor does not request or intentionally collect

The App does not require an account and does not ask you to provide your name, email address, password, or other account credentials.

For diagnostics, Phone Doctor does not intentionally collect or store:

  • IMEI or device serial number
  • MAC address
  • Your photo, video, music, or document library
  • Your precise or approximate location
  • The content of calls, SMS messages, contacts, or other personal communications

Advertising-related data may be processed directly by Google through the Google Mobile Ads SDK as described in Section 7.

3. Permissions and hardware access

Phone Doctor requests sensitive runtime permissions only when a feature needs them:

  • Camera — used when you open Camera Test to preview the camera and read supported camera capabilities. Phone Doctor does not save or upload camera photos or video as part of the diagnostic flow.
  • Microphone — used when you run Microphone Test. A short temporary recording may be created in the App's private cache to measure signal characteristics. It remains on-device and is deleted by the diagnostic flow.
  • Bluetooth Connect — on Android versions that require it, used only when Bluetooth state/details are requested in Connectivity Lab. It is not used by Phone Doctor to infer your location.
  • Notifications — requested only if you enable a feature that needs health-check/reminder notifications.

The App also declares normal Android permissions such as Internet, network state, Wi-Fi state, NFC, and vibration where required for diagnostics or advertising. These normal permissions do not expose your personal files to Phone Doctor.

4. Storage, sensors, thermal and performance diagnostics

  • Storage: the App reads basic device storage capacity/usage information available through Android. It does not request broad media-library or external-storage read access. Performance testing may create a temporary test file inside the App's private cache, measure local sequential read/write performance, and delete that file afterward.
  • Memory: RAM diagnostics use system memory information and bounded in-app memory benchmark buffers.
  • CPU / Performance: user-initiated benchmark work runs locally on the device and may temporarily increase CPU utilization.
  • Thermal: supported Android thermal APIs are used to display thermal state/headroom where the device exposes them.
  • Sensors: Phone Doctor reads available sensor metadata and live samples locally for diagnostic display. Missing optional sensors are treated as device capability information, not personal data.
  • Display, touch, speaker, vibration, flashlight and hardware buttons: these tests operate locally and do not upload test interactions.

5. Connectivity Lab and network probe

Connectivity Lab may display device/network state such as Wi-Fi availability, mobile connectivity capability, Ethernet, VPN, NFC, Bluetooth, DNS servers, estimated link information, and connected USB device descriptors where Android makes those details available.

The optional network probe runs only when you request it. It resolves example.com and attempts a short TCP connection to port 443 to measure basic DNS and TCP connection latency. It is not a bandwidth upload test and does not send your diagnostic history, microphone recordings, reports, or personal files.

6. Reports and sharing

Phone Doctor can create diagnostic report text/PDF files in app-controlled storage. A report is shared outside the App only when you explicitly choose a destination through Android's system share interface.

The App also offers an optional share flow connected to the rewarded-ad feature. Phone Doctor opens Android's share sheet, but the App does not verify whether the selected destination actually publishes or sends the shared message.

7. Advertising, consent and rewarded ads

The free version of Phone Doctor may display banner, interstitial, and rewarded advertisements using Google AdMob / Google Mobile Ads SDK. Google may process technical and advertising-related information, which can include device or advertising identifiers, IP address, ad interactions, and other data described by Google's own policies.

Where required, Phone Doctor uses Google User Messaging Platform (UMP) to request/manage advertising consent before ads are requested. When Google requires privacy options to remain available, the App provides access to those choices from Settings.

The rewarded-ad feature can grant one hour without Phone Doctor banner and interstitial ads after the ad reward is earned. The expiry time for that temporary reward is stored locally in the App's private settings.

Google advertising information: Google advertising technologies. Google privacy information: Google Privacy Policy.

8. Third-party services

Phone Doctor integrates Google's Mobile Ads SDK and Google's User Messaging Platform for advertising and consent. The App does not currently integrate Firebase Analytics, a developer-operated analytics platform, or a developer-operated crash-reporting backend.

This Privacy Policy page itself is hosted using GitHub Pages. Visiting this web page may result in GitHub processing ordinary web-request information under GitHub's own privacy practices. This is separate from Phone Doctor's on-device diagnostic processing.

9. Data retention and deletion

Diagnostic history and app settings are kept in app-private storage on your device until you clear them or uninstall the App. Where available, you can clear diagnostic history from Phone Doctor's Settings. Android system settings can also be used to clear the App's local data.

Temporary diagnostic files, such as microphone or storage benchmark files, are intended to be removed after their associated diagnostic operation finishes.

10. Your choices

  • You may deny or revoke Camera, Microphone, Bluetooth, or Notification permissions through Android Settings.
  • You may choose not to run optional network/performance tests.
  • You may choose not to share a report or app-sharing message.
  • Where UMP privacy choices are available, you can reopen them from Phone Doctor Settings.
  • You can clear locally stored history or uninstall the App to remove app-private local data.

11. Children's privacy

Phone Doctor is a general-audience device utility and is not specifically directed to children. The App does not require users to create a personal account or submit profile information to the developer.

12. Security

Phone Doctor minimizes data exposure by keeping diagnostic processing and history local wherever possible and by using Android app-private storage for its own files. No software can guarantee absolute security, but the App is designed to avoid unnecessary collection of personal information.

13. Changes to this policy

If Phone Doctor's data practices or integrated services change, this page will be updated and the "Last updated" date above will be revised. The current version will remain available at this same privacy-policy URL.
